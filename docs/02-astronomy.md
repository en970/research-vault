# Astronomy & space science

Part of [research-vault](../README.md). 60 entries, verified 2026-08-28 — free status and limits change, so check the source before you depend on it.

Beginner rating: 5/5 means a newcomer gets value in ten minutes; 1/5 means specialist tooling and patience.

## Data

| Resource | What it is | Access | Free | Beginner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [**AAVSO (American Association of Variable Star Observers)**](https://www.aavso.org) | The AAVSO International Database holds over a century of variable-star photometry contributed by amateurs and professionals; the companion VSX index catalogues over 2 million known variable stars. *Membership is paid but is NOT required to access data, submit observations, or propose new variables to VSX; registration (free) is required for submissions.* | Light Curve Generator and data download on aavso.org; VSX search at vsx.aavso.org (also mirrored in VizieR); submit your own observations via WebObs after obtaining a free observer code. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**ALeRCE alert broker**](https://alerce.science) | Astronomical alert broker ingesting the ZTF stream and Rubin/LSST alerts (one of the official Rubin brokers), with machine-learning classifications, light curves and cross-matches for millions of transients and variables. *Alternatives with similar free access include Fink (fink-broker.org), Lasair and ANTARES; all serve the world-public Rubin alert stream, so no data rights are needed for alerts.* | Web explorer at alerce.online; Python client `pip install alerce` then `from alerce.core import Alerce`; public HTTP API. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**ALMA Science Archive**](https://almascience.org) | All ALMA observations become public after a one-year proprietary period; the archive serves raw visibilities and, increasingly, imaged data products for thousands of projects. *Reprocessing raw visibilities requires CASA plus substantial disk and CPU; look for archival imaged products (continuum/line cubes) first, or use the ARI-L/ALMINER value-added products.* | Query interface at almascience.org/aq (no login to search or download public data); `astroquery.alma` for scripted download. | Free | 2/5 |
| [**ASAS-SN Sky Patrol**](https://asas-sn.osu.edu) | All-Sky Automated Survey for Supernovae; Sky Patrol V2.0 serves continuously updated V- and g-band light curves for 111 million catalogued targets, with photometry typically available within about an hour of observation. *Shallow depth (small telephoto lenses) - roughly 17-18 mag limit; ideal for bright variables, supernovae and long baselines rather than faint sources.* | Web query at asas-sn.osu.edu (arbitrary-position photometry) and the V2.0 interface at asas-sn.ifa.hawaii.edu/skypatrol/ with a Python client (pyasassn; docs at asas-sn.ifa.hawaii.edu/documentation). | Free | 4/5 |
| [**Breakthrough Listen Open Data Archive**](https://breakthroughinitiatives.org/opendatasearch) | Multi-petabyte public archive of radio (and some optical) SETI observations from the Green Bank Telescope, Parkes and the Automated Planet Finder, the largest open SETI dataset in existence. *Individual filterbank/HDF5 files are often several GB and formats require specialized tools; start with the tutorial datasets rather than raw voltages.* | Search and direct download at breakthroughinitiatives.org/opendatasearch (beta interface at seti.berkeley.edu/opendata); read files with `pip install blimpy`; Doppler-drift searches with `turbo_seti`. | Free | 2/5 |
| [**CHIME/FRB Catalogs**](https://www.chime-frb.ca/catalog) | CHIME/FRB Catalog 2 (2026) contains 4,539 fast radio bursts from 3,641 unique sources detected between July 2018 and September 2023, an 8.5-fold increase over Catalog 1 (536 FRBs, 2021). *Raw voltage/intensity data are not fully public; the catalogs contain burst properties and selected waterfall data.* | Browse and download via the catalog site; machine-readable data and utilities via the open-data portal chime-frb-open-data.github.io and `pip install cfod`. | Free | 3/5 |
| [**DESI Data Archive**](https://data.desi.lbl.gov) | Public data from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument; DR1 (March 2025) contains spectra and redshifts for ~18.7 million galaxies, quasars and stars. *Full-release files are very large; SPARCL or Astro Data Lab queries are the practical route on a laptop.* | Direct HTTPS download at data.desi.lbl.gov; individual spectra retrievable without bulk download via the SPARCL service (`pip install sparclclient`) at NOIRLab. | Free | 2/5 |
| [**DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys**](https://www.legacysurvey.org) | Deep grz imaging and source catalogues from DECam, BASS and MzLS built for DESI targeting; the sky viewer serves image cutouts of any position on demand. *Cutout service is rate-limited for bulk use; for large jobs download bricks directly or query Data Lab.* | Cutout API, e.g. https://www.legacysurvey.org/viewer/cutout.fits?ra=180.0&dec=30.0&layer=ls-dr10&pixscale=0.262 (also cutout.jpg); interactive viewer at legacysurvey.org/viewer; catalogues queryable via NOIRLab Astro Data Lab TAP. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**e-CALLISTO**](https://www.e-callisto.org) | Worldwide network of low-cost CALLISTO radio spectrometers monitoring solar radio bursts around the clock; daily FITS spectrograms from all stations are collected centrally and archived publicly. *Data quality varies by station (radio-frequency interference); the network also welcomes new hosts - the spectrometer hardware is intentionally low-cost, making this a practical instrument route for individuals and small institutions.* | Browse and download gzipped FITS files at e-callisto.org/Data/data.html; daily quicklook plots and automatic burst lists on the same site. | Free | 3/5 |
| [**ESA Gaia Archive**](https://gea.esac.esa.int/archive/) | Gaia DR3 (2022) gives positions, parallaxes, proper motions and photometry for ~1.8 billion sources; DR4, covering 5.5 years of data at ~400 TB including epoch-level data, is scheduled for 2 December 2026. *Anonymous queries have row/job limits; a free account lifts them and keeps query history. ESA has announced breaking interface changes to the archive in preparation for DR4's much larger volume.* | ADQL queries in the web archive, or `pip install astroquery` then `from astroquery.gaia import Gaia; Gaia.launch_job('SELECT ...')`; bulk files via direct download. Subsets also served by CDS, AIP and other partner centres. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**ESO Science Archive**](https://archive.eso.org) | Raw and pipeline-processed data from the VLT/VLTI, VISTA, VST, La Silla telescopes and APEX; observations become public after the proprietary period (typically one year), and processed science-ready collections are served alongside raw frames. *Some services (large raw-data requests, calSelector) work best with a free ESO User Portal account.* | Science portal at archive.eso.org/scienceportal; programmatic access via `astroquery.eso` and TAP; direct download of processed data products. | Free | 3/5 |
| [**Euclid Q1 Data Release**](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/euclid/q1-data) | Euclid Quick Release 1 (19 March 2025): ~30 TB of VIS/NISP imaging, slitless spectra and catalogues over ~63 deg2 of the Euclid Deep Fields North, Fornax and South plus LDN1641. *Q1 is single-visit depth over deep fields only; substantially larger cosmology-scale releases are planned as the survey progresses.* | ESA Euclid Science Archive (linked from the Q1 data page); full mirror at IRSA with TAP/ADQL catalog queries and AWS cloud copies; tutorial notebooks at caltech-ipac.github.io/irsa-tutorials. | Free | 3/5 |
| [**Fermi Science Support Center**](https://fermi.gsfc.nasa.gov/ssc/) | All Fermi-LAT photon data have been public immediately since August 2009, with continuous all-sky coverage; GBM burst data and the 4FGL source catalogs are also served. *No registration for data, but LAT likelihood analysis has a steep learning curve and needs several GB of spacecraft/diffuse files.* | LAT data server web query at fermi.gsfc.nasa.gov/ssc/; analysis with fermitools (conda install from the fermi channel) and `pip install fermipy`. | Free | 2/5 |
| [**GWOSC (Gravitational Wave Open Science Center)**](https://gwosc.org) | Public LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA strain data and event catalogues (through GWTC-4, released 2025, covering O4a), plus tutorials and the annual Open Data Workshop materials. *Strain data are released after a proprietary period, so the most recent observing-run segments lag public availability.* | Event portal and bulk downloads at gwosc.org; `pip install gwpy` then `TimeSeries.fetch_open_data('H1', t0, t1)`; the `gwosc` and `pycbc` packages for catalog queries and analysis. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**HEASARC**](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov) | NASA's archive for X-ray and gamma-ray astronomy: Chandra, XMM-Newton, Swift, NuSTAR, NICER, IXPE, Fermi and dozens of historical missions, plus the LAMBDA CMB data holdings. *Data are free with no gates, but high-energy analysis (response files, effective areas) has real overhead; Swift and NICER are the gentlest entry points.* | Xamin/Browse query tools at heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov; `astroquery.heasarc`; analysis with the free HEASoft suite (XSPEC etc.); Chandra-specific tools via the CXC (cxc.cfa.harvard.edu, CIAO software). | Free | 2/5 |
| [**Helioviewer**](https://helioviewer.org) | Browse, animate and export solar imagery from SDO, SOHO, STEREO and other missions, spanning decades of observations, in a zoomable web interface backed by a public API. *Serves browse-quality JPEG2000 images; for science-grade SDO FITS files use the JSOC export system (jsoc.stanford.edu) or SunPy's Fido search, both free.* | Web app at helioviewer.org; REST API at api.helioviewer.org; Python wrapper `pip install hvpy`. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**IceCube public data releases**](https://icecube.wisc.edu/science/data-releases/) | Public neutrino datasets including IceTracks-DR2 (released May 2026): 14 years of track-like neutrino candidates from April 2008 to May 2022 for point-source searches, with documentation and example analyses. *These are curated event lists and instrument response functions, not raw detector data; questions go to analysis@icecube.wisc.edu.* | Direct download from the data-releases page (files hosted on Harvard Dataverse); CSV/HDF5 formats readable with pandas. | Free | 2/5 |
| [**JPL Solar System Dynamics (Horizons & SBDB)**](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov) | Horizons computes high-precision ephemerides for essentially every known solar-system body plus spacecraft; the Small-Body Database serves orbital and physical parameters with close-approach data. | Web app at ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/; REST APIs at ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov (no key needed); scripted access via `astroquery.jplhorizons` and `astroquery.jplsbdb`. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**MAST (Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes)**](https://archive.stsci.edu) | STScI archive holding JWST, HST, TESS, Kepler/K2, GALEX and Pan-STARRS data; all observations become public after any proprietary period (typically 12 months for JWST/HST GO programs) and public holdings are mirrored to AWS. *A free MyST account is only needed to download proprietary-period data you are authorized for; everything public is anonymous-access.* | Web portal at mast.stsci.edu, or `pip install astroquery` then `from astroquery.mast import Observations`; cloud reads via `Observations.enable_cloud_dataset()` against s3://stpubdata (anonymous, no AWS account needed). | Free | 4/5 |
| [**Minor Planet Center**](https://www.minorplanetcenter.net) | The IAU's official clearinghouse for astrometric observations and orbits of over 1.4 million minor planets and comets; publishes orbit files (MPCORB), observation databases and NEO confirmation lists. *Submitting observations requires obtaining an observatory code (free, demonstrated astrometric accuracy required) - a long-standing amateur-professional pathway, now also the public route for Rubin solar-system discoveries.* | Web services and database queries at minorplanetcenter.net; bulk MPCORB downloads; astrometry submissions accepted from anyone observing from a site with an MPC observatory code. | Free | 3/5 |
| [**NASA Exoplanet Archive**](https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu) | Vetted database of confirmed exoplanets and their measured parameters, with linked literature references; listed 6,128 confirmed planets as of February 2026 (the 6,000 milestone was passed in September 2025). *The companion ExoFOP service (exofop.ipac.caltech.edu) coordinates follow-up of TESS and K2 candidates; browsing is open and contributing observations needs only a free account - a genuine route for amateurs with modest telescopes.* | Interactive tables in the browser; TAP/API queries; `pip install astroquery` then `from astroquery.ipac.nexsci.nasa_exoplanet_archive import NasaExoplanetArchive`. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**NASA Planetary Data System (PDS)**](https://pds.nasa.gov) | Permanent archive of data from NASA planetary missions (Mars orbiters and rovers, Cassini, New Horizons, Juno, OSIRIS-REx and more), organized in discipline nodes with peer-reviewed data standards. *Navigation across nodes takes practice; the SPICE toolkit (NAIF, naif.jpl.nasa.gov; Python wrapper `pip install spiceypy`) is the free standard for the geometry files that accompany most datasets.* | Search at pds.nasa.gov; direct HTTP download from discipline-node servers; image-focused browsing via the Cartography and Imaging Sciences node (PDS Imaging Atlas). | Free | 3/5 |
| [**NED (NASA/IPAC Extragalactic Database)**](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu) | Cross-matched multiwavelength positions, redshifts, photometry, images and literature for hundreds of millions of extragalactic objects, fused from major sky surveys and journal tables. *Complements SIMBAD: NED is deeper for galaxies and redshift bookkeeping; SIMBAD is stronger for Galactic objects.* | Web interface at ned.ipac.caltech.edu; `from astroquery.ipac.ned import Ned`; TAP and object-lookup APIs. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**Rubin Observatory / LSST data**](https://rubinobservatory.org/for-scientists/data-products/data-access) | Data Preview 1 (2025) covers seven ~1 deg2 commissioning fields; Early Data Preview 2 (2026) adds LSSTCam observations from April 2025 to January 2026; the real-time alert stream (design rate ~10 million alerts/night) has been flowing to brokers since February 2026. *RSP accounts and pixel/catalogue releases are restricted to data-rights holders (US- and Chile-affiliated researchers plus named international in-kind contributors) until releases become world-public after a delay; unaffiliated researchers can still do real science today via the public alert stream and MPC.* | Rubin Science Platform (data.lsst.cloud) notebooks and TAP for data-rights holders; alerts are world-public through brokers (ALeRCE, Fink, Lasair, ANTARES and others) with no data rights needed; solar-system detections flow to the Minor Planet Center. | Free, application | 2/5 |
| [**SDOML (SDO Machine Learning Dataset)**](https://sdoml.github.io) | Curated, calibration-corrected SDO dataset for machine learning: AIA images 2010-2020 in 10 wavebands at 512x512 and 6-minute cadence, HMI vector magnetograms at 12-minute cadence, and EVE irradiance 2010-2014, all in cloud-friendly Zarr format. *Downscaled from full 4096x4096 resolution by design; for full-resolution science data go to JSOC instead.* | Read directly from AWS S3 with zarr/fsspec (bucket listed at registry.opendata.aws/sdoml-fdl); loaders and examples at sdoml.github.io and github.com/SDOML. | Free | 2/5 |
| [**SDSS SkyServer**](https://skyserver.sdss.org) | SQL interface to all Sloan Digital Sky Survey releases; DR20 (30 July 2026, third SDSS-V release) adds over 3 million optical spectra from the Black Hole Mapper and Milky Way Mapper programs. *CasJobs and SciServer compute require a free email registration; SkyServer quick queries have row/time limits.* | Free-form SQL in the browser at skyserver.sdss.org; longer jobs via CasJobs (free account); `astroquery.sdss` for scripted access; flat files from data.sdss.org. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**SIMBAD**](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr) | CDS reference database resolving names, cross-identifications, basic measurements and complete literature links for over 10 million astronomical objects outside the solar system. *Part of the wider free CDS ecosystem: the Aladin interactive sky atlas (aladin.cds.unistra.fr, desktop and Lite web version) and the CDS X-Match service for crossmatching billion-row catalogues.* | Web queries at simbad.cds.unistra.fr; `from astroquery.simbad import Simbad`; TAP service for bulk cross-identification. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**SPHEREx Archive at IRSA**](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/Missions/spherex.html) | NASA's SPHEREx (launched March 2025) is performing the first all-sky 0.75-5 micron spectral survey in 102 bands; IRSA publishes new Quick Release spectral images weekly (QR2, with improved calibration, since October 2025), with data also mirrored to AWS. *QR2 supersedes QR1; higher-level products (full-sky maps, catalogues) roll out over the mission's remaining years.* | IRSA search tools, TAP/ADQL queries and the SPHEREx Source Discovery and Mosaic tools; cloud access via the public AWS bucket (registry.opendata.aws/spherex-qr); documentation at caltech-ipac.github.io/spherex-archive-documentation. | Free | 3/5 |
| [**Transient Name Server (TNS)**](https://www.wis-tns.org) | The IAU's official registry for reporting and naming astronomical transients; essentially all supernova discoveries and classifications flow through it, searchable by anyone. *A working route for amateurs who discover transients: register, report with required astrometry/photometry, and the object receives an official designation.* | Public web search without login; reporting discoveries or classifications requires a free registered account (individual or group); rate-limited REST API with an API key for bots. | Free, email | 3/5 |
| [**VizieR**](https://vizier.cds.unistra.fr) | CDS library of more than 20,000 published astronomical catalogues and tables, from small journal tables to full surveys, all queryable through uniform Virtual Observatory interfaces. *Default query row limits are low (50); set Vizier.ROW_LIMIT = -1 in astroquery to retrieve full tables.* | Web queries at vizier.cds.unistra.fr; `pip install astroquery` then `from astroquery.vizier import Vizier`; TAP endpoint for ADQL (tapvizier.cds.unistra.fr). | Free | 4/5 |
| [**ZTF (Zwicky Transient Facility) public data**](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/Missions/ztf.html) | Northern-sky time-domain survey; bimonthly public data releases of images and light curves are served at IRSA, and the real-time alert stream is redistributed publicly. *Partnership-time data are proprietary; the public surveys are fully open. Light-curve queries scale poorly for millions of objects - use the bulk release files for statistics work.* | IRSA web tools and light-curve API; `astroquery.ipac.irsa` for scripted queries; alerts via community brokers (ALeRCE, Fink, Lasair, ANTARES) or bulk alert archive at ztf.uw.edu. | Free | 3/5 |

## Software

| Resource | What it is | Access | Free | Beginner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [**Astropy**](https://www.astropy.org) | The core Python package for astronomy: coordinates, WCS, FITS and table I/O, units, time systems and cosmology; foundation of virtually the entire Python astronomy ecosystem of affiliated packages. *BSD-licensed, community-developed; affiliated packages (photutils, specutils, regions, ccdproc) cover most reduction tasks.* | `pip install astropy` (or conda); documentation at docs.astropy.org. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**astroquery**](https://astroquery.readthedocs.io) | One Python package with uniform interfaces to dozens of archives and services - MAST, Gaia, SIMBAD, VizieR, IRSA, NED, ESO, ALMA, JPL Horizons, HEASARC and more - so a laptop can script against most of the world's astronomy data. *Individual services keep their own rate limits and occasional required credentials; module namespaces changed in recent versions (e.g. astroquery.ipac.ned), so match examples to your installed version.* | `pip install astroquery`; e.g. `from astroquery.simbad import Simbad; Simbad.query_object('M31')`. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**Lightkurve**](https://docs.lightkurve.org) | Python package for Kepler, K2 and TESS photometry: one-line search and download from MAST, detrending, periodograms and transit searches with well-documented tutorials. *Runs comfortably on a laptop for single targets; for bulk work use the TIKE cloud platform where the same data live next to the compute.* | `pip install lightkurve`; e.g. `import lightkurve as lk; lk.search_lightcurve('Pi Mensae', mission='TESS').download().plot()`. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**REBOUND**](https://rebound.readthedocs.io) | Open-source N-body integrator (IAS15, WHFast and other symplectic schemes) used across planetary dynamics and planetesimal studies; runs real research problems on a laptop. | `pip install rebound`; add `reboundx` for extra forces (GR, radiation pressure, migration). | Free | 4/5 |
| [**Skyfield**](https://rhodesmill.org/skyfield/) | Pure-Python astronomy engine computing positions of planets, comets, asteroids and Earth satellites directly from JPL ephemerides and MPC/Celestrak data, with results consistent with Horizons. *Downloads ephemeris files on first use (tens of MB); ideal for observation planning, satellite passes and occultation work without any web service dependency.* | `pip install skyfield`; e.g. load('de421.bsp') then observe planets from any topocentric location. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**Stellarium**](https://stellarium.org) | Open-source desktop planetarium rendering a realistic sky with large star catalogues, telescope control, and observation planning; also available as Stellarium Web in the browser. *Mobile apps are paid; desktop and web versions are fully free.* | Direct download (Windows/macOS/Linux, GPL) from stellarium.org; browser version at stellarium-web.org. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**SunPy**](https://sunpy.org) | Core Python library for solar data analysis: the Fido interface searches and downloads from VSO, JSOC and other archives, with Map and TimeSeries classes for images and light curves. *JSOC bulk exports ask for a (free) registered email address.* | `pip install sunpy`; e.g. `from sunpy.net import Fido, attrs as a; Fido.search(a.Time(...), a.Instrument.aia)`. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**TOPCAT**](https://www.star.bristol.ac.uk/mbt/topcat/) | Desktop Java tool for interactive work on large tables: million-row crossmatching, sky and 3D plotting, and built-in Virtual Observatory access (TAP, cone search) to services like VizieR and Gaia; actively maintained (v4.10-6, May 2026). *Handles tables of millions of rows on modest hardware; the standard answer to 'how do I crossmatch two catalogues without writing code'.* | Download the topcat jar from the website and run `java -jar topcat-full.jar`; command-line sibling STILTS for scripted pipelines. | Free | 4/5 |

## Literature

| Resource | What it is | Access | Free | Beginner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [**arXiv astro-ph**](https://arxiv.org/archive/astro-ph) | The astrophysics preprint server, running since 1992; near-universal practice in the field means the freely readable preprint version exists for the large majority of current astronomy papers. *First-time submitters without an institutional email may need an endorsement from an established arXiv author - a real but usually surmountable hurdle for independent researchers.* | Read free at arxiv.org (six astro-ph subcategories); daily listings and RSS; submission is free with an arXiv account. | Free | 5/5 |

## Publishing

| Resource | What it is | Access | Free | Beginner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [**Astronomy & Astrophysics (A&A)**](https://www.aanda.org) | Major astronomy journal, fully open access since 2022 under the Subscribe-to-Open model - 2026 is the fifth consecutive year with no article processing charges for authors and free reading for everyone. *S2O is renewed annually and depends on library subscriptions; A&A has said recent thresholds were met only narrowly, so the no-APC status should be re-checked each year. | [**JOSS (Journal of Open Source Software)**](https://joss.theoj.org) | Free, developer-friendly peer-reviewed journal for research software; astronomy packages routinely publish here to make their code citable, with open review conducted on GitHub. *The software itself must be open-source with a substantial scholarly effort; small scripts are rejected as out of scope.* | Submit a repository with a short paper.md via joss.theoj.org; review happens in a public GitHub issue; no charges. | Free, email | 3/5 |
| [**Research Notes of the AAS (RNAAS)**](https://journals.aas.org/research-notes/) | Venue for brief communications (max 1,500 words, one figure or table): free to read, currently no author charge, each note gets a DOI and ADS indexing and is typically published within ~72 hours of receipt. *Not peer reviewed (editor-checked only) and not copy-edited - suitable for negative results, follow-up observations, code/data announcements; a practical first publication route for individuals and amateurs.* | Submit via the AAS journals submission system (AAS membership not required); LaTeX/Word templates provided. | Free, email | 4/5 |
| [**The Open Journal of Astrophysics**](https://astro.theoj.org) | Peer-reviewed arXiv-overlay journal covering astrophysics and cosmology: submission, refereeing and publication are all free of charge, funded by Maynooth Academic Publishing. *No APC and no reader paywall; indexed and citable. A realistic venue for researchers without APC funds.* | Post your paper to arXiv, then submit the arXiv ID through astro.theoj.org for peer review. | Free, email | 3/5 |

## Community

| Resource | What it is | Access | Free | Beginner |

| [**Astronomy Stack Exchange**](https://astronomy.stackexchange.com) | Question-and-answer site for astronomy with a substantial archive of answered questions, from observational practice to astrophysics calculations, answered by a mix of professionals and knowledgeable amateurs. *For software-specific questions, the astropy tag here and the OpenAstronomy forum overlap; research-level questions sometimes fare better on Physics Stack Exchange.* | Read without an account; free registration to ask or answer. | Free | 5/5 |
