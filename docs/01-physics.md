# Physics

Part of [research-vault](../README.md). 53 entries, verified 2026-08-28 — free status and limits change, so check the source before you depend on it.

Beginner rating: 5/5 means a newcomer gets value in ten minutes; 1/5 means specialist tooling and patience.

## Data

| Resource | What it is | Access | Free | Beginner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [**ATLAS Open Data**](https://opendata.atlas.cern) | ATLAS's dedicated open-data site with two tracks: laptop-sized education datasets with Jupyter tutorials, and the 2024 research release of ~65 TB of 13 TeV proton-proton collision data from 2015-2016 (over 7 billion events, plus ~2 billion simulated events) under CC0. *The education datasets are simplified and not suitable for publishable measurements; the 2024 research release is, and has documentation aimed at people outside the collaboration.* | Web interface; education notebooks run in the browser; research-grade PHYSLITE files served through the CERN Open Data Portal and readable with `pip install uproot`. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**BIPM Key Comparison Database (KCDB)**](https://www.bipm.org/kcdb/) | The official record of world metrology under the CIPM Mutual Recognition Arrangement: 26,000+ calibration and measurement capabilities, ~1,278 key comparisons and ~777 supplementary comparisons from 260 participating institutes across nine measurement areas. *Reference material for anyone doing precision measurement — tells you the state of the art and its uncertainty for a given quantity — but it is a registry, not a bulk dataset.* | Free web search (quick and advanced) over CMCs and comparison reports; comparison final reports downloadable as PDF. | Free | 2/5 |
| [**CERN Open Data Portal**](https://opendata.cern.ch) | Public archive of collision data, simulations, software environments, and documentation from CERN experiments including ALICE, ATLAS, CMS, DELPHI, LHCb, OPERA, and TOTEM. CMS alone has released roughly 5 PB of real and simulated data since 2014; every dataset gets a DOI and an open licence (mostly CC0). *Research-grade datasets run to hundreds of GB or TB. On a laptop, work with derived formats (CMS NanoAOD, ATLAS PHYSLITE) and stream files with uproot/XRootD rather than downloading everything.* | Web interface with direct downloads; bulk retrieval via `pip install cernopendata-client`; analysis environments ship as Docker images and CVMFS repositories so old software still runs. | Free | 3/5 |
| [**CODATA Fundamental Physical Constants (NIST)**](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/Constants/) | The internationally recommended values of the fundamental physical constants with uncertainties and correlations. The CODATA 2022 adjustment is the current set as of 2026. *A new CODATA adjustment lands every four years — check which set your software version ships before high-precision work.* | Searchable web interface and downloadable ASCII table; also available programmatically as `scipy.constants` in the SciPy library. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**FAIR-MAST**](https://mastapp.site) | UKAEA's open archive of diagnostic data from the MAST spherical tokamak (campaigns M05-M09), served as cloud-optimised Zarr and Parquet from a public S3 store with a REST/GraphQL metadata API — one of the very few openly available real tokamak diagnostic datasets, widely used for fusion ML work. *Raw diagnostic signals with minimal hand-holding; expect to invest time understanding tokamak diagnostics. Related releases appear on the UKAEA Open Data portal (opendata.ukaea.uk).* | REST and GraphQL API at mastapp.site; bulk data from the public S3 endpoint (s3.echo.stfc.ac.uk); code and docs at github.com/ukaea/fair-mast. | Free | 2/5 |
| [**HEPData**](https://www.hepdata.net) | Repository of the numerical data behind published particle-physics results — cross-section tables, exclusion limits, correlation matrices — built on the Durham database accumulated over four decades and actively fed by ATLAS, CMS, LHCb and others. Hosted at CERN infrastructure, run from Durham/IPPP. *Coverage depends on collaborations depositing their tables; recent LHC results are well covered, older or smaller experiments patchier.* | Web interface; every table downloads as CSV, YAML, JSON, or ROOT; records are query-able via a REST/JSON API; submissions are prepared with `pip install hepdata-lib`. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**IAEA Nuclear Data Services**](https://nds.iaea.org) | International clearing-house for nuclear data: the EXFOR experimental reaction database (over 21,000 experiments, over 11 million data points), mirrors of major evaluated libraries, and the LiveChart of Nuclides for browsing ENSDF structure and decay data graphically. *Interfaces are dated but functional; the same EXFOR content is also mirrored by NNDC and the OECD NEA.* | Web interfaces; EXFOR web retrieval with plotting; LiveChart at nds.iaea.org/relnsd/vcharthtml/VChartHTML.html; most datasets downloadable in standard formats. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**LXCat**](https://www.lxcat.net) | Community platform of contributed databases of electron- and ion-scattering cross sections and measured swarm/transport parameters for low-temperature plasma modelling, with an online Boltzmann-equation solver for computing electron energy distributions and rate coefficients. *Each contributed database has its own citation requirement — cite the specific database, not just LXCat. Contributor accounts are only needed to upload data.* | Web interface: browse, plot, and download cross-section sets as plain text; no account needed to download. | Free | 3/5 |
| [**Materials Project**](https://materialsproject.org) | DOE-funded database of DFT-computed structures, band structures, elastic, dielectric and thermodynamic properties covering over 150,000 inorganic compounds plus ~170,000 molecules; roughly 600,000 registered users as of 2025. *All data is computed (GGA/GGA+U and r2SCAN DFT), not experimental — band gaps in particular carry systematic errors. Registration is free but required for the web app and API.* | Web app after free registration; API key from your dashboard, then `pip install mp-api` and use MPRester; bulk data also on public AWS S3 buckets with no account. | Free, api-key | 3/5 |
| [**National Nuclear Data Center (NNDC)**](https://www.nndc.bnl.gov) | Brookhaven's nuclear data hub: ENSDF evaluated nuclear structure and decay data, the ENDF/B-VIII.1 evaluated reaction library (released August 2024), the NuDat 3 interactive chart of nuclides, XUNDL, and the NSR bibliography. *Processed application libraries (ACE files for MCNP etc.) are distributed separately via LANL/RSICC channels, some of which require registration.* | Web interfaces (NuDat 3 for browsing, ENDF retrieval forms); ENDF/B-VIII.1 available as direct download in ENDF-6 and GNDS formats. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**NIST Atomic Spectra Database**](https://www.nist.gov/pml/atomic-spectra-database) | Critically evaluated wavelengths, energy levels, transition probabilities and ionization energies for atoms and atomic ions — the standard reference for spectral-line identification in physics, plasma diagnostics and astrophysics. *Cite the database version and retrieval date; there is no official bulk download of the entire database.* | Web query forms at physics.nist.gov/asd with tab-delimited/CSV output for batch use. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**NIST Chemistry WebBook**](https://webbook.nist.gov) | Evaluated thermochemical, thermophysical and ion-energetics data plus IR, UV/Vis and mass spectra for thousands of chemical species, including high-accuracy fluid properties (REFPROP-grade equations of state) for common gases and liquids. *Sits on the physics-chemistry boundary; for experimental physicists it is the fastest free route to vapour pressures, heat capacities and reference spectra.* | Web interface with search by name, formula, CAS number; per-species data pages with downloadable spectra. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**OQMD — Open Quantum Materials Database**](https://oqmd.org) | Northwestern University database of DFT-calculated thermodynamic and structural properties containing about 1.4 million entries (1,407,395 as of August 2026), released under CC-BY 4.0. *Like all high-throughput DFT sets, formation energies are reliable for ranking but individual values need convergence checks before publication-grade claims.* | Web search at oqmd.org; RESTful and OPTIMADE APIs; full database downloadable as a MySQL dump; `qmpy` Python package for local queries. | Free | 3/5 |
| [**Particle Data Group (PDG)**](https://pdg.lbl.gov) | The Review of Particle Physics: evaluated masses, widths, branching ratios and limits for all known particles, plus ~100 review articles on topics from the Standard Model to statistics. The 2026 edition is free online; pdgLive gives interactive access to every measurement with references. *The Python API is labelled beta by PDG — cross-check values against pdgLive before using them in a publication. The printed book/booklet costs money; all content is free online.* | pdgLive web interface at pdg.lbl.gov; machine-readable access via `pip install pdg` (docs at pdgapi.lbl.gov); summary tables and data files as direct downloads. | Free | 5/5 |

## Software

| Resource | What it is | Access | Free | Beginner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [**Geant4**](https://geant4.web.cern.ch) | The standard open-source C++ toolkit for simulating the passage of particles through matter, used across high-energy physics, medical physics, radiation protection and space instrumentation. *Serious learning investment (C++, physics lists, geometry description); many detector simulations run fine on a laptop. Free user forum and annual courses; documentation is thorough.* | Source and binary downloads from geant4.web.cern.ch; build with CMake; extensive examples included in the distribution. | Free | 1/5 |
| [**Kwant**](https://kwant-project.org) | Open-source Python package for tight-binding quantum transport: conductance, band structures, edge states and scattering problems in mesoscopic and topological systems. *Development pace is slow but the package is stable and still the community standard for this class of problem; the topocondmat.org course teaches it in context.* | `conda install -c conda-forge kwant` (or pip on supported platforms); tutorial in the online documentation. | Free | 3/5 |
| [**LAMMPS**](https://www.lammps.org) | Sandia's open-source (GPL) classical molecular dynamics code for soft matter, solid-state and coarse-grained simulations, scaling from a single laptop core to the largest supercomputers. *Force-field choice is the hard part; the mailing list and MatSci forum are active.* | Prebuilt binaries, `conda install -c conda-forge lammps`, or source from lammps.org; driven by plain-text input scripts. | Free | 2/5 |
| [**MadGraph5_aMC@NLO**](https://launchpad.net/mg5amcnlo) | Automated tool for computing tree-level and NLO cross sections and generating parton-level events for arbitrary Standard Model and BSM processes; the workhorse of LHC phenomenology. *LO studies of simple processes run comfortably on a laptop; NLO with matching/merging gets computationally heavy. Pair with Pythia for showering.* | Download the tarball from launchpad.net/mg5amcnlo or `git clone https://github.com/mg5amcnlo/mg5amcnlo`; drive it from its own command shell; needs Python 3.7+ and gfortran for NLO. | Free | 2/5 |
| [**openQCD**](https://luscher.web.cern.ch/luscher/openQCD/) | Martin Lüscher's GPL-licensed lattice QCD simulation package (HMC/SMD algorithms, up to 2+1+1 flavours), a reference code of the lattice community; version 2.4.2 released April 2026. *Production lattices need HPC allocations, but small test lattices run on a workstation and the code doubles as a masterclass in simulation algorithms.* | Source tarball from the website; C code parallelised with MPI; full algorithmic documentation included. | Free | 1/5 |
| [**PennyLane**](https://pennylane.ai) | Xanadu's open-source (Apache-2.0) Python framework for differentiable quantum programming — variational circuits, quantum chemistry and quantum ML — with automatic differentiation and interfaces to PyTorch, JAX and multiple hardware backends. *Simulators run locally; hardware backends require accounts with the respective providers.* | `pip install pennylane`; large collection of free demos and codebook exercises on pennylane.ai. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**PYTHIA**](https://pythia.org) | The most widely used general-purpose event generator for high-energy collisions (pp, e+e-, and more), covering hard processes, parton showers, multiparton interactions, hadronization and decays. Current series is Pythia 8.3, GPL v2+. *Physics choices (tunes, PDF sets) matter for results; the online manual is the authoritative reference.* | Source tarball from pythia.org; C++ library with Python interface; builds on a laptop in minutes. | Free | 2/5 |
| [**Qiskit**](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/qiskit) | IBM's open-source (Apache-2.0) SDK for building, transpiling and running quantum circuits, with the high-performance Aer simulator for local execution and the qiskit-ibm-runtime client for real IBM hardware. *The SDK and simulator are fully free with no account; only hardware execution requires IBM registration (see IBM Quantum Platform entry).* | `pip install qiskit qiskit-aer` for local simulation; add `qiskit-ibm-runtime` plus a free IBM account to run on real QPUs. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**Quantum ESPRESSO**](https://www.quantum-espresso.org) | GPL-licensed suite for plane-wave density-functional theory: electronic structure, phonons, and materials modelling at the nanoscale — one of the two dominant open-source DFT codes alongside ABINIT. *Small-cell calculations run on a laptop; converged production work often needs a cluster. Commercial competitor VASP is licensed/paid — QE is the standard free route.* | Source download from quantum-espresso.org or `conda install -c conda-forge qe`; pseudopotential libraries freely downloadable from the same site. | Free | 2/5 |
| [**QuTiP**](https://qutip.org) | Open-source Python library for simulating the dynamics of open quantum systems (master equations, Monte Carlo trajectories, arbitrary time-dependent Hamiltonians), standard in quantum optics, superconducting circuits and trapped ions. Version 5.3.1 released August 2026; NumFOCUS-affiliated. *Simulations of ~10-20 qubits or modest Hilbert spaces are laptop-friendly; memory grows exponentially with system size.* | `pip install qutip`; extensive tutorial notebooks at qutip.org. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**ROOT**](https://root.cern) | CERN's C++ data-analysis framework — histogramming, fitting (RooFit), I/O for the .root format in which essentially all LHC data is stored — with Python bindings (PyROOT). Open source (LGPL), in continuous development since 1995. *Steep learning curve; if you only need to read .root files in Python, uproot (Scikit-HEP) is far lighter. The ROOT Forum (root-forum.cern.ch) is an active help channel answered by developers.* | `conda install -c conda-forge root`, prebuilt binaries from root.cern, or distro packages; `import ROOT` from Python. | Free | 2/5 |
| [**Scikit-HEP**](https://scikit-hep.org) | Community ecosystem of Python packages for particle physics: uproot reads and writes ROOT files with no ROOT installation, awkward handles jagged arrays, plus hist, vector, particle, and iminuit. The practical route into LHC open data for anyone who knows NumPy. *Pure-Python stack that runs anywhere; for very large datasets you still need to think about chunked/streamed reading.* | `pip install uproot awkward hist vector particle`; each package documented at scikit-hep.org. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**SciPy ecosystem**](https://scipy.org) | NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib — the numerical backbone under nearly every Python-based physics tool listed here, including special functions, ODE solvers, optimisation, FFTs and the CODATA constants in scipy.constants. | `pip install numpy scipy matplotlib`; documentation and tutorials at scipy.org. | Free | 5/5 |

## Literature

| Resource | What it is | Access | Free | Beginner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [**arXiv**](https://arxiv.org) | The spine of physics literature since 1991: over 2.4 million preprints across physics, maths and allied fields, with essentially every particle-physics, cond-mat and quantum paper appearing here first. Reading is free with no account. *First-time submitters need endorsement per category. An institutional email can auto-endorse; unaffiliated authors must ask an established arXiv author (endorsers are flagged on abstract pages) to endorse them — workable but a real hurdle, so line up an endorser before you need one.* | Web, plus free bulk/API access (arXiv API, Kaggle dataset, full-text bulk on S3); submission requires a free account. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**INSPIRE-HEP**](https://inspirehep.net) | The high-energy physics community's bibliographic database: about 1.88 million literature records (August 2026) with citation graphs, author profiles, conference and jobs listings; run by a collaboration including CERN, DESY, Fermilab, IHEP, IN2P3 and SLAC. *Coverage is deep for HEP, nuclear and astro-particle physics but not for condensed matter or general physics — use it for what it is.* | Web search (powerful query syntax); free REST API documented at github.com/inspirehep/rest-api-doc — no key required. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**Living Reviews in Relativity**](https://link.springer.com/journal/41114) | Fully open-access review journal on general relativity and gravitational physics whose invited articles are periodically updated by their authors — the standard entry points into topics like tests of GR, numerical relativity, and gravitational-wave theory. *Articles are commissioned — this is a reading resource, not a submission venue for most researchers.* | All articles free to read/download on SpringerLink. | Free | 3/5 |

| [**IBM Quantum Platform (Open Plan)**](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com) | Free access to IBM's superconducting quantum processors (100+ qubit systems): the Open Plan grants 10 minutes of QPU runtime per 28-day rolling window, and a March 2026 promotion lets active Open Plan users opt in to an additional 180 minutes spread over 12 months. *10 minutes is QPU execution time, not wall-clock — enough for real small-scale experiments if you simulate first and batch jobs. ## Publishing

| Resource | What it is | Access | Free | Beginner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [**Quantum (journal)**](https://quantum-journal.org) | Non-profit, community-run arXiv-overlay journal for quantum science founded in 2017. Default publication fee is €600 (since January 2025), but authors can pay a reduced €100 or request a full waiver with no justification required — roughly a quarter of papers use the waiver. *Selective venue with strong reputation in quantum information; the no-questions-asked waiver makes it genuinely open to unfunded authors.* | Submit the arXiv version through quantum-journal.org; published papers are free to read (CC-BY) and live on arXiv. | Free, email | 3/5 |
| [**SciPost**](https://scipost.org) | Academic-run diamond OA publisher founded in 2016: no fees for authors or readers, open (published) peer review. Flagship SciPost Physics plus SciPost Physics Core, Lecture Notes, Proceedings and Codebases; funded by voluntary institutional sponsorships at an average cost of about €500 per paper. *SciPost Physics has become a respected venue in theory and cond-mat/quantum; refereeing standards are high and acceptance is selective.* | Submit via scipost.org (arXiv-linked workflow); all published papers and referee reports free on the site. | Free, email | 3/5 |
| [**SCOAP3**](https://scoap3.org) | CERN-coordinated consortium of 3,000+ libraries and agencies in 44 countries that pays open-access costs centrally so authors pay nothing. Phase 4 (2025-2027) covers 11 journals including JHEP, EPJC, Physics Letters B, Nuclear Physics B, PTEP, Chinese Physics C, and HEP articles in PRL, PRC and PRD. *Only high-energy-physics articles qualify in the APS journals (coverage per journal ranges from ~9% to 100%); outside HEP the scheme does not apply.* | No action needed: publish HEP content in a participating journal and OA fees are covered automatically; articles come out CC-BY with authors retaining copyright. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**Zenodo**](https://zenodo.org) | CERN-operated general-purpose repository (launched 2013, built on InvenioRDM with OpenAIRE/EU backing): free DOIs for datasets, software, posters, and preprints from any discipline, with versioning and a GitHub integration that archives tagged releases automatically. *Standard way to make supplementary data and analysis code citable when a journal or field archive has no home for it.* | Web upload after free registration; REST API with personal access token for scripted deposits; default limit 50 GB per record (more on request). | Free, email | 5/5 |

## Learning

| Resource | What it is | Access | Free | Beginner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [**David Tong — Lectures on Theoretical Physics**](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/teaching.html) | Complete, free PDF lecture notes from Cambridge covering most of the theory curriculum — classical dynamics, electromagnetism, statistical physics, quantum mechanics, quantum field theory, string theory, solid state, general relativity, gauge theory and more — widely treated as de facto textbooks. *Notes assume the mathematical maturity of a physics degree; the QFT and Statistical Physics sets are the community's most-recommended free treatments of those subjects.* | Direct PDF download, no registration. | Free | 4/5 |

| [**IBM Quantum Learning**](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/learning/en) | IBM's free course platform (successor to the Qiskit Textbook): 10+ courses including John Watrous's 'Basics of Quantum Information', plus hands-on Qiskit tutorials that run against simulators or real hardware. *Content is Qiskit/IBM-centric by design; concepts transfer, tooling is vendor-specific.* | Browse free on the web; a free IBM account links courses to executable notebooks and the Open Plan hardware tier. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**MIT OpenCourseWare — Physics**](https://ocw.mit.edu/search/?d=Physics) | Free, openly licensed materials for MIT physics courses from introductory mechanics through graduate quantum field theory, many with complete video lectures, problem sets and exams (e.g. 8.04 Quantum Physics I, 8.05/8.06, 8.033 Relativity). *Materials are static snapshots of past course offerings — no instructor interaction or certificates.* | Direct browsing and download at ocw.mit.edu; videos also on YouTube; no registration. | Free | 5/5 |

| [**PIRSA — Perimeter Institute Recorded Seminar Archive**](https://pirsa.org) | Free, permanent, citable archive of recorded physics seminars and courses from the Perimeter Institute — every talk gets a PIRSA number — including the complete Perimeter Scholars International graduate lecture courses across quantum gravity, quantum information, cosmology, condensed matter and mathematical physics. *Research-level content; the PSI course collections are the most structured entry point.* | Stream free at pirsa.org, browsable by field and series; no registration. | Free | 3/5 |
| [**The Feynman Lectures on Physics**](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu) | Caltech's free, high-quality HTML edition of all three volumes of the Feynman Lectures — mechanics, electromagnetism and matter, and quantum mechanics — the field's most famous introduction to physical reasoning. *Online reading only — there is no authorized free PDF/ebook download; print editions remain commercial.* | Read free in the browser at feynmanlectures.caltech.edu. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**The Theoretical Minimum**](https://theoreticalminimum.com) | Leonard Susskind's Stanford Continuing Studies lecture series for people who 'know a bit of algebra and calculus': full video courses on classical mechanics, quantum mechanics, relativity, cosmology, statistical mechanics and black holes. *Companion books are commercial; the videos are complete on their own.* | Watch free at theoreticalminimum.com or on the Stanford Institute for Theoretical Physics YouTube channel. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**Topology in Condensed Matter**](https://topocondmat.org) | Free open-source graduate course on topological phenomena in condensed matter (Majoranas, topological insulators, quantum Hall physics), created by researchers at TU Delft, Maryland and Leiden for edX in 2015 and maintained since, combining theory with runnable Kwant simulations. *Assumes solid quantum mechanics and some band theory.* | Read and run everything at topocondmat.org, no registration; source notebooks on GitHub. | Free | 3/5 |
