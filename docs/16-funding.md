## Publishing

### [ORCID](https://orcid.org/)

`Free, email` · beginner 5/5 · persistent researcher identifier

Free, persistent identifier for individual researchers, maintained by a global not-for-profit; records can be created, edited and maintained free of charge and integrate with journal submission systems, funder and grant application systems, repositories and research information systems.

**Access.** Register at orcid.org, add works and affiliations, then supply the iD in every manuscript, dataset deposit and grant form. A free public API is available for reading records.

**Caveats.** Costs nothing to individuals; the paid membership tiers are for institutions and publishers integrating the API. For unaffiliated researchers this is the single cheapest piece of infrastructure that makes a publication record portable and machine-readable — it disambiguates you from name-alikes and survives every change of institution or none at all.

## Funding

### [1517 Fund — Medici Project](https://www.1517fund.com/medici/)

`Free, application` · beginner 5/5 · no-strings micro-grants for students and dropouts

Micro-grants of at least $1,000, paid with no strings attached, to high-school and college students, dropouts and deep-tech scientists working on a self-directed project — explicitly not projects done for grades or admissions.

**Access.** Email any team member at firstname@1517fund.com with a five-minute Loom video: who you are, why you care about the project, and what the money unlocks.

**Caveats.** Effectively North America only — the fund states international applicants may get limited support because of regulatory constraints. Amounts are small and this is a talent-scouting pipeline for a venture fund, so expect the framing to favour buildable artefacts over scholarly output.

### [ACL Diversity & Inclusion and virtual subsidies](https://2026.aclweb.org/)

`Free, application` · beginner 3/5 · conference cost relief (NLP)

The Association for Computational Linguistics runs D&I subsidies and separate virtual-attendance subsidies for its main conference; both calls were announced for ACL 2026 (April 2026).

**Access.** Calls are posted on each year's conference site with their own forms and deadlines; the virtual subsidy is the low-friction option when travel is impossible.

**Caveats.** Amounts, eligibility and deadlines are set fresh each year by that year's D&I chairs and are not stable across conferences — read the specific call rather than a previous year's terms. Sibling conferences (EMNLP, NAACL, EACL) run comparable schemes under the same umbrella.

### [ACX Grants](https://www.astralcodexten.com/archive?sort=search&search=grants)

`Free, application` · beginner 4/5 · irregular open-call micro-grants

Grant rounds run by Scott Alexander on the Astral Codex Ten blog, funding individual projects across science, public health, forecasting and software. Results posts confirm rounds in 2021, 2022 (Grants++), 2024 and 2025; the most recent results post is 'ACX Grants Results 2025' (13 October 2025).

**Access.** Rounds are announced as blog posts with a Google Form; watch the blog or subscribe to the RSS feed. Past rounds also ran an associated impact market / mini-grants track.

**Caveats.** Cadence is irregular and there is no standing application portal — no open round was visible on the blog as of 2026-08-28, and a June 2025 post ('Open Questions For Future ACX Grants Rounds') suggests the format is still being reconsidered. Grants are small and one-off; the strongest secondary benefit is visibility to the blog's donor-heavy readership.

### [AIMS (African Institute for Mathematical Sciences)](https://aims.ac.za/)

`Free, application` · beginner 3/5 · fully funded master's in mathematical sciences

An intensive one-year postgraduate structured master's in mathematical sciences taught across three semesters by visiting lecturers, run on a full-scholarship model within a pan-African student community.

**Access.** Apply through the AIMS prospective-students pages; the network operates centres in several African countries, each with its own intake.

**Caveats.** What the scholarship covers in detail (tuition, accommodation, meals, travel) is not stated on the programme pages checked on 2026-08-28 — email the centre and get it in writing before you commit. Intake is aimed at African graduates in mathematical sciences; the pace is demanding and the programme is residential.

### [Astera Residency](https://www.astera.org/program/residency/)

`Free, application` · beginner 2/5 · fully funded independent research residency

A 12–18 month fully funded residency for people building public-goods research projects in neuroscience, AI, AI-enabled life sciences and open science: salary of $125,000–$250,000 depending on experience, a project budget of up to $1.5M, and compute access via Voltage Park (24,000 NVIDIA H100s plus reserved B200/GB300 capacity).

**Access.** Open online application when a cycle is live; cycles run roughly twice a year, so check the residency page periodically.

**Caveats.** Residents work in person at Astera's Emeryville, California hub, which means relocation and a US work authorisation problem for most international applicants. Outputs must be committed to open and accessible release. Extremely competitive and scoped to Astera's focus areas — a strong track record is assumed.

### [Breakthrough Junior Challenge](https://breakthroughjuniorchallenge.org/)

`Free, application` · beginner 5/5 · student science-communication prize

Annual global science-video competition for high-school students, with a $250,000 post-secondary scholarship for the winner, a $50,000 prize for their teacher and a $100,000 Breakthrough Science Lab for their school. The current cycle closes 15 September 2026 (peer review due 30 September 2026).

**Access.** Submit a short explanatory video on a physics, life-sciences or mathematics concept through the challenge site; entrants must also complete a peer-review round on other submissions.

**Caveats.** High-school students only — this is not a route for graduate students or independents, and it is included here because it is one of the very few genuinely open, genuinely large science prizes. Single winner per year against a global field, so odds are extremely long.

### [Chevening Scholarships](https://www.chevening.org/)

`Free, application` · beginner 3/5 · national scholarship scheme (UK)

UK government scholarship scheme funding one-year master's degrees in the UK for candidates from a long list of eligible countries and territories, covering tuition, living costs and travel.

**Access.** Single online application through chevening.org during the annual window (typically opening in August and closing in early November for entry the following autumn); you apply to Chevening and to universities separately.

**Caveats.** Requires prior work experience and a commitment to return to your home country after the degree; eligible countries change year to year, so check the country list rather than assuming. Selection weights leadership and career trajectory heavily, not just academic record. The scheme did not respond to automated checks on 2026-08-28 — confirm current dates and the country list on the site.

### [CIMPA](https://www.cimpa.info/)

`Free, application` · beginner 3/5 · research schools in developing countries

International association that has spent nearly fifty years running mathematics research schools in developing countries, alongside Partnership Schools, CIMPA Fellowships and CIMPA–ICTP Research in Pairs.

**Access.** Two routes: propose and organise a school through the organisers' candidacy portal, or apply as a participant to a specific school listed on the site.

**Caveats.** CIMPA's money mainly goes to the organisers of a school, who then decide participant support — whether your travel and local costs are covered depends on the individual school, so ask its organisers directly. Site content is partly in French. Mathematics and mathematical-sciences focus.

### [Code for Science & Society Event Fund](https://www.codeforsociety.org/eventfund)

`Free, application` · beginner 3/5 · community event and workshop grants

Community-governed fund that invests directly in events building data-science skills and social infrastructure in under-served communities; past cohorts funded open-science training, teacher data literacy and infectious-disease research meetings.

**Access.** Application through the call published on the Event Fund page; CS&S also acts as a fiscal sponsor for open-science projects.

### [CZI Essential Open Source Software for Science (EOSS)](https://chanzuckerberg.com/rfa/essential-open-source-software-for-science/)

`Free, application` · beginner 2/5 · grants for maintenance of scientific software

The most significant dedicated funder of maintenance work on open-source scientific software: Cycle 6 was $11.7M in partnership with the Kavli Foundation and Wellcome, with awards of $50,000–$200,000 per year for two years ($100,000–$400,000 total).

**Access.** Historically a letter of intent followed by a full proposal, submitted through CZI's grants portal by the software project's maintainers.

**Caveats.** Dormant, and this matters: Cycle 6 closed on 17 October 2023 and no successor cycle has been announced. As of 2026-08-28 all 28 RFAs listed on CZI's science funding page were marked closed. Join the CZI science mailing list rather than waiting on the page. Awards were made to institutions or fiscal sponsors, not to individuals directly.

### [DAAD Scholarship Database](https://www2.daad.de/deutschland/stipendium/datenbank/en/21148-scholarship-database/)

`Free` · beginner 5/5 · national scholarship database (Germany)

Free, filterable database of scholarships for international students, graduates and researchers coming to Germany, showing 149 scholarship options in the English interface, filterable by your country of residence, status and subject.

**Access.** Web interface, no account needed — set your country of residence and status, then read each programme's own terms and deadline.

**Caveats.** The country filter refers to where you currently live and apply from, not your nationality; DAAD advises contacting them if you are applying from elsewhere. The database also indexes non-DAAD funders, whose entries DAAD does not guarantee — check terms with the programme itself. Monthly stipend amounts are on the individual programme pages, not the search results.

### [DrivenData](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/)

`Free (registration), email` · beginner 4/5 · social-impact prize competitions

Machine-learning competitions with social-impact sponsors including NASA, Microsoft, Meta AI and the World Bank. Open competitions as of 2026-08-28 include Trace the Ace ($50,000 in prizes), Lost in Transcription ($20,000) and the DaT Parkinson's Challenge (€25,000).

**Access.** Register, download the competition data, and submit predictions against the leaderboard; practice competitions with no prizes are available for learning.

**Caveats.** Fewer, longer competitions than Kaggle, which means less crowding but also fewer chances per year. Prize eligibility, licensing of winning code and any country restrictions are set per competition — read the individual rules. Winning solutions are usually required to be open-sourced, which is a plus for a public record but a constraint if you wanted to commercialise.

### [EA Funds — Transformative AI Fund](https://funds.effectivealtruism.org/funds/transformative-ai)

`Free, application` · beginner 4/5 · rolling grants to individuals

Always-open fund making grants typically in the $10k–$150k range (rarely above $300k) to individuals, new organisations and existing organisations starting a new project, explicitly aiming to be 'early-stage, fast, and tolerant of failure'.

**Access.** Rolling online application form on the fund page; contact transformativeai [at] effectivealtruismfunds.org with questions.

**Caveats.** This fund replaced the Long-Term Future Fund, which EA Funds closed — old LTFF links now redirect here, so guides that still name the LTFF are out of date. Scope is restricted to work on risks and impacts from transformative AI; a general independent-research salary request outside that scope will not fit.

### [Emergent Ventures](https://www.mercatus.org/emergent-ventures)

`Free, application` · beginner 5/5 · fast micro-grants, no affiliation required

Rolling grant programme at the Mercatus Center (George Mason University) for individuals with a specific project; the only stated eligibility bar is being 13 or older, and applicants worldwide are accepted, with dedicated tracks for India, Africa, the Caribbean and Ukraine selectable on the form.

**Access.** Single web application form linked from the programme page; a short project description plus a CV. No institution, no budget template, no letters of reference.

**Caveats.** Grant sizes are not published; historically they run from a few thousand dollars to low six figures, and the programme does not publish decision rates or deadlines. Decisions are made by one person and are idiosyncratic — treat it as a lottery ticket with a very cheap ticket, not a plan. No progress reports are required, but also no support structure.

### [Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters](https://www.eacea.ec.europa.eu/scholarships/emjmd-catalogue_en)

`Free, application` · beginner 4/5 · funded international master's programmes

EU-funded catalogue of 220 joint master's programmes delivered by consortia across several countries, with scholarships available for students from anywhere in the world.

**Access.** Search the catalogue, then apply directly to the consortium running the programme you want — applications typically run October to January for the following academic year, and each consortium sets its own process.

**Caveats.** The catalogue is updated annually and not every listed programme still carries EU scholarships — some are at the end of their funding period or have kept the Erasmus Mundus name after funding ended, so confirm scholarship availability with the consortium before applying. What the scholarship covers varies by programme. Multi-country study means multiple visa processes.

### [Ethereum Foundation Ecosystem Support Program](https://esp.ethereum.foundation/)

`Free, application` · beginner 3/5 · open-source and cryptography research grants

Grant programme funding free and open-source public goods for the Ethereum ecosystem, including cryptography, protocol and applied-security research. Published allocations: 677 projects / $44.4M in 2024, 498 / $61.1M in 2023, 397 / $30.0M in 2022.

**Access.** Apply through the Wishlist and RFP listings on the ESP site; a non-financial 'Office Hours' track gives feedback on project ideas before you apply.

**Caveats.** Domain-restricted: your work has to plausibly benefit Ethereum, so this suits cryptography, distributed systems, formal methods, mechanism design and privacy researchers and nobody else. Individual grant sizes are not published. Payment is often in crypto, which creates tax and accounting work.

### [EU Funding & Tenders Portal](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/portal/screen/home)

`Free` · beginner 3/5 · funding opportunity database (EU)

Single entry point for European Union funding calls, including Horizon Europe, the European Research Council and Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions, with full call documents, work programmes and evaluation criteria published openly.

**Access.** Browse and search calls without logging in; an EU Login account and a participant identification code are required to submit a proposal.

**Caveats.** Almost all calls require a legal-entity applicant, usually with a host organisation, so an unaffiliated individual cannot submit alone. The portal is dense and the terminology is its own dialect — start from the topic's work programme document. Reading past evaluation criteria and funded-project abstracts here is free competitive intelligence even if you never apply.

### [Experiment](https://experiment.com/)

`Free (registration), email` · beginner 4/5 · crowdfunding for research projects

Crowdfunding platform built specifically for scientific projects: a researcher publishes a hypothesis, method and itemised budget, and backers fund it directly. It is one of the very few funding routes that does not ask what institution you are at.

**Access.** Web interface — draft a project, pass editorial review, then run a time-limited campaign and drive your own traffic to it.

**Caveats.** The platform deducts a percentage fee plus payment processing from funds raised; the fee pages returned HTTP 403 to automated checks on 2026-08-28, so read the current terms yourself before you set a target. Success is a function of the audience you already have — campaigns without an existing mailing list, lab network or press hook routinely fail, and typical raises are in the low thousands, not grant-scale.

### [Falling Walls Lab](https://falling-walls.com/lab/)

`Free, application` · beginner 4/5 · pitch competition with travel to a global final

Three-minute pitch competition for students and early-career professionals across all disciplines, run as dozens of local Labs worldwide; 100 Lab winners advance to the Global Finale in Berlin each November to compete for the Emerging Talents Breakthrough title.

**Access.** Apply to a local Lab in your region through the Lab locations page; each Lab sets its own deadline.

**Caveats.** Whether travel and accommodation to Berlin are covered is set by the organisers of each individual Lab and by the central programme — confirm with your local Lab before assuming a funded trip. The payoff is visibility and network rather than research money.

### [Foresight Institute Fellowship](https://foresight.org/fellowship/)

`Free, application` · beginner 3/5 · non-salaried early-career fellowship

Year-long fellowship for early-career scientists and engineers in secure AI, longevity biotechnology, nanotechnology, neurotechnology and 'existential hope', providing introductions to senior researchers and funders, paid travel to one technical workshop and one Vision Weekend, seminar membership and quarterly career calls.

**Access.** Open application on the fellowship page; as of 2026 the site was taking applications for a forthcoming cohort.

**Caveats.** No stipend or research budget is disclosed — the value is network and travel, not salary, so it does not replace income. Focus areas are narrow and the community skews toward Bay Area / Berlin technologists.

### [Fulbright Foreign Student Program](https://foreign.fulbrightonline.org/)

`Free, application` · beginner 3/5 · national scholarship scheme (US)

US government programme funding non-US citizens to undertake graduate study or research in the United States; applications are made in your home country rather than centrally.

**Access.** Apply to the Fulbright Commission/Foundation or the US Embassy in your home country — the central site's role is to route you to your national commission, which publishes its own deadlines, quotas and terms.

**Caveats.** What is funded, how many awards exist and when applications close all vary by country, so a figure quoted for one country tells you nothing about yours. Many national programmes carry a two-year home-residency requirement after the award. US federal funding for exchange programmes has been volatile in recent years; confirm the current cycle with your national commission before building plans around it.

### [GitHub Sponsors](https://github.com/sponsors)

`Free, email` · beginner 4/5 · recurring support for research software maintainers

Recurring or one-off sponsorship of open-source maintainers, supported in over 100 regions. GitHub charges no fee on sponsorships from personal accounts, so 100% reaches the developer; sponsorships from organisation accounts carry up to 6% (3% card processing, which invoiced billing removes, plus 3% service fee).

**Access.** Enable a Sponsors profile on your GitHub account, add sponsor tiers, and surface the Sponsors button on your repositories; payouts go to a bank account or Stripe Connect in a supported region.

**Caveats.** You must reside in a supported region to receive funds, which excludes a number of countries. The GitHub Sponsors Matching Fund is closed (applications after 1 January 2020 are not eligible), so there is no longer a match. Realistic income for a scientific package with a small user base is tens of dollars a month; this funds coffee and CI, not a salary, unless the project is widely depended on.

### [Google Summer of Code](https://summerofcode.withgoogle.com/)

`Free, application` · beginner 4/5 · paid mentored open-source contribution

Paid, mentored programme placing newcomers on open-source projects for a 12+ week engagement, with project sizes of roughly 90, 175 or 350 hours; many participating organisations are scientific software communities. Stipends are paid on passing evaluations.

**Access.** Pick a participating organisation, engage with its mentors before the window, then submit a project proposal through the GSoC site during the contributor application period.

**Caveats.** Open to anyone 18+ who is a student OR an open-source beginner (fewer than about ten prior contributions), eligible to work in their country of residence, and accepted at most once before; residents of US-embargoed countries are excluded. Stipend amounts vary by project size and country tier and are not published on the FAQ — check the current programme rules. Competition for slots at popular organisations is heavy and proposals written without prior contact with mentors rarely succeed.

### [Grants.gov](https://www.grants.gov/)

`Free` · beginner 4/5 · funding opportunity database (US federal)

The single official listing of US federal discretionary funding opportunities across all agencies, searchable free of charge by keyword, agency, eligibility category and deadline.

**Access.** Free web search with no account; an account plus a SAM.gov registration for the applicant entity is required to submit. Saved searches can email you when matching opportunities post.

**Caveats.** The site explicitly does not publish personal financial assistance — it is aimed at organisations, and most opportunities require an entity registration that takes weeks. A minority of notices list 'individuals' as an eligible applicant type; filter for those if you are unaffiliated. Non-US applicants are ineligible for most, though some agencies permit foreign institutions. Useful even when you cannot apply: reading funded solicitations tells you exactly how a US agency frames a problem.

### [HFSP Postdoctoral Fellowships](https://www.hfsp.org/funding/hfsp-funding/postdoctoral-fellowships)

`Free, application` · beginner 2/5 · international postdoctoral fellowship

Three-year, non-renewable fellowships in the life sciences with a living allowance, a research and travel allowance, and where applicable child, parental-leave and relocation allowances. Two tracks: Long-Term Fellowships for biology PhDs and Cross-Disciplinary Fellowships for PhDs from physics, chemistry, mathematics, engineering and computer science moving into biology.

**Access.** Two-stage application on the HFSP portal. For the 2027 cycle: LOI portal opens 12 March 2026, LOI initiation 5 May 2026, LOI submission 12 May 2026, full proposal 24 September 2026, outcome March 2027, fellowship starts between 1 April 2027 and 1 January 2028.

**Caveats.** Nationality is unrestricted but mobility is mandatory: you cannot hold the fellowship in your country of nationality, and you must not have lived, studied or worked in the proposed host country for more than 12 months before it starts. An approved host institution and named supervisor are required at application, so the host search has to happen a year ahead. Allowance amounts vary by host country and are not fixed globally.

### [ICML Financial Aid](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/FinancialAid)

`Free, application` · beginner 4/5 · conference cost relief

Two award types for ICML 2026: an in-person award covering conference registration plus a seven-night hotel stay, and a virtual award covering virtual registration, aimed at people for whom attending would cause financial burden.

### [ICTP (Abdus Salam International Centre for Theoretical Physics)](https://www.ictp.it/)

`Free, application` · beginner 3/5 · programmes for scientists from developing countries

Trieste-based centre whose whole mandate is supporting scientists from developing countries, running the Postgraduate Diploma, master's programmes, PhD and sandwich-PhD arrangements, an Associateship scheme for repeat research visits, postdoctoral positions, laboratory placements and a large annual calendar of schools and workshops.

**Access.** Browse the Opportunities section and apply per programme; schools and workshops each publish their own application with a support request box.

**Caveats.** The level of support differs sharply by activity — some schools cover travel and local costs for participants from developing countries, others only waive fees — so read the specific event page rather than assuming. Applications are competitive and typically require references from senior scientists.

### [ICTP Postgraduate Diploma Programme](https://www.ictp.it/opportunity/ictp-postgraduate-diploma-programme)

`Free, application` · beginner 3/5 · fully funded pre-PhD year

One academic year (1 September–31 August) in Trieste, Italy, in high energy/cosmology/astroparticle physics, condensed matter and statistical physics, mathematics, Earth system physics, or quantitative life sciences. Around ten scholarships per stream per year for candidates from developing countries, covering full tuition, an annual stipend and a travel grant.

**Access.** Online application via ICTP's e-applications portal with transcripts and degrees in English, English-proficiency evidence and two references from senior scientists; questions to diploma@ictp.it.

**Caveats.** Eligibility is tightly drawn: generally under 28, an MSc (or an exceptionally strong BSc) in physics, mathematics or a related field, and citizenship of a UN, UNESCO or IAEA member state. Self-funded candidates may attend if they can prove adequate resources. This is the strongest single bridge from an under-resourced undergraduate education into a competitive PhD.

### [Idea Wild](https://ideawild.org/apply/)

`Free, application` · beginner 5/5 · equipment grants for conservation researchers

Long-running programme (over 30 years) that provides field equipment rather than cash to conservation and biodiversity researchers, sourced through partnerships with suppliers including Vortex Optics, Burris Optics and Wildlife Acoustics. Applications are currently accepted from all countries.

**Access.** Online application form on the Idea Wild site listing the specific equipment you need and how it fits the project; questions to ideawild@ideawild.org.

This is the fastest realistic route for a student in a low-income country who needs a camera trap, GPS unit or recorder and has no departmental budget.

### [Kaggle Competitions](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions)

`Free (registration), email` · beginner 4/5 · prize competitions as income

Free-to-enter machine-learning competitions, some sponsored with cash prize pools, alongside research and community competitions with no money attached. For an unaffiliated researcher this is one of the few routes where a laptop plus the free notebook tier can convert directly into both money and a citable public result.

**Access.** Create an account, accept the competition rules, then submit predictions or notebooks against a held-out leaderboard; free CPU/GPU/TPU notebook quotas are provided.

**Caveats.** Most competitions carry no prize, and prize eligibility is governed per-competition — several exclude residents of sanctioned countries, require identity verification, and impose licence terms on winning solutions (sometimes including transferring rights to the sponsor). Read the rules tab before investing weeks. Expected value for a first-time entrant is close to zero money and considerable learning.

### [Ko-fi](https://ko-fi.com/)

`Freemium, email` · beginner 5/5 · direct reader support for research programmes

Lightweight page for one-off tips, monthly memberships, commissions and digital-file sales, usable by an independent researcher who publishes a blog, dataset or newsletter and wants readers to pay for it directly.

**Access.** Web interface — create a page, connect Stripe or PayPal, and link it from your writing. A free account plus an optional paid 'Gold' tier with extra features.

**Caveats.** Payment-processor fees always apply on top of whatever the platform charges; verify the current fee schedule before promising sponsors anything. Patreon is the larger alternative with more membership tooling but a higher platform cut. Income here tracks audience size, so it is a supplement to a research programme, not a foundation for one.

### [Manifund](https://manifund.org/)

`Free, email` · beginner 4/5 · regranting and open funding proposals

Public platform where anyone can post a funding proposal and have it funded by donors or by 'regrantors' — domain experts given delegated budgets. The site describes grants processed in days rather than months, and all proposals, comments and payouts are public.

**Access.** Create an account, publish a project page with budget and milestones, then solicit regrantors and donors directly. Impact-certificate rounds are also run periodically.

**Caveats.** Funded work must fit a US charitable purpose (Manifund acts as fiscal sponsor). In practice the funded portfolio skews heavily toward AI safety, biosecurity and effective-altruism-adjacent projects; a proposal outside those areas is unlikely to find a regrantor. Everything you write is public, including a rejected ask.

### [Marie Skłodowska-Curie Postdoctoral Fellowships](https://marie-sklodowska-curie-actions.ec.europa.eu/actions/postdoctoral-fellowships)

`Free, application` · beginner 2/5 · EU postdoctoral fellowship

Horizon Europe fellowships covering a living allowance, mobility allowance, family and special-needs allowances where applicable, plus research, training and networking costs. European Postdoctoral Fellowships run 1–2 years in an EU member state or associated country; Global Postdoctoral Fellowships run 2–3 years with 1–2 years outside Europe and a mandatory one-year return phase.

**Access.** Submitted jointly with a host organisation through the EU Funding & Tenders Portal; the annual call has a single deadline, usually in early autumn.

**Caveats.** You need a PhD at the call deadline and at most eight years of post-PhD research experience (career breaks and non-research work are discounted). The mobility rule bites hardest: you must not have resided or worked in the host country for more than 12 months in the 36 months before the deadline, which rules out staying where you already are. A willing host supervisor is a prerequisite, so this is not a route for someone with no academic contacts. Success rates are typically well under one in five.

Grants for conservation of individual species — plants, animals and fungi — awarded without regional or taxonomic discrimination, prioritising critically endangered, endangered and data-deficient species. 3,274 grants have been awarded since 2009.

## Community

### [NASA Citizen Science](https://science.nasa.gov/citizen-science/)

`Free, email` · beginner 5/5 · recognition through volunteer research

Catalogue of 46 active NASA citizen-science projects open to anyone; more than 650 NASA citizen scientists have co-authored peer-reviewed publications, making this a documented route to authorship without any institutional affiliation.

**Access.** Pick a project from the catalogue and join through its own site; most need only a smartphone or laptop, a few (asteroid occultations, exoplanet transits) want a telescope. Subscribe to updates by emailing do-nasa-science-join@lists.nasa.gov with 'Subscribe' in the subject.

**Caveats.** Co-authorship is real but not automatic: it depends on sustained, substantial contribution and on each project team's authorship policy. There is no stipend — this buys credibility and a publication record, not income.

### [Zooniverse](https://www.zooniverse.org/about)

`Free, email` · beginner 5/5 · crowdsourcing platform and authorship route

The largest people-powered research platform, with projects spanning astronomy, biology, climate, history and physics, and a long record of peer-reviewed publications produced from volunteer classifications since 2007.

**Access.** Volunteer on any project with a free account. Researchers can build their own project at no cost with the Zooniverse Project Builder, submit it for review, launch it, then download the aggregated classifications.

**Caveats.** The Project Builder is the underrated half: an unaffiliated researcher with an image or transcription dataset can get thousands of human labels for free, which no budget would otherwise buy. Projects must pass Zooniverse review before launch, and attracting volunteers to a new project requires active community work — a launched project is not a staffed one.

*Also listed under: astronomy.*
