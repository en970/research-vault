# Medicine & health sciences

Part of [research-vault](../README.md). 54 entries, verified 2026-08-28 — free status and limits change, so check the source before you depend on it.

Beginner rating: 5/5 means a newcomer gets value in ten minutes; 1/5 means specialist tooling and patience.

## Data

| Resource | What it is | Access | Free | Beginner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [**All of Us Researcher Workbench**](https://www.researchallofus.org) | NIH's All of Us program (target: one million US participants) provides survey, EHR, genomic and wearable data through a cloud Researcher Workbench, with $300 in computational credits per researcher. *Only researchers affiliated with academic, healthcare, nonprofit or government organizations can register — independent researchers are currently excluded (NIH says broader access is planned). Registered vs Controlled tiers depend on the institution's agreement; compute beyond the $300 credit is billed to a Google account.* | Your institution signs a Data Use and Registration Agreement (free), then you register: Google 2-step verification, identity check via Login.gov/ID.me, a 30-90 minute ethics training, and the Data User Code of Conduct. | [**CDC WONDER**](https://wonder.cdc.gov) | CDC's online query system for US public health data: detailed mortality (1968-present, ICD-coded), natality, cancer incidence, vaccine adverse events (VAERS) and more, aggregated by cause, geography, demographics and time. *Aggregate tables only, not microdata; cells based on fewer than 10 deaths are suppressed, and sub-national data carry use restrictions you must accept. The XML API is workable but clunky.* | Web query interface with CSV export; programmatic access by POSTing XML request blocks to the same database endpoints (documented per database). | Free | 5/5 |
| [**ClinicalTrials.gov**](https://clinicaltrials.gov) | US/global registry of over 500,000 clinical studies with protocols, eligibility criteria, locations and, for many trials, deposited summary results. Machine-readable via the modern v2 API. *Rate limits are not officially documented (community reports suggest roughly 50 requests/minute — throttle politely). Registry entries reflect what sponsors report; results deposition lags and is incomplete. WHO ICTRP aggregates additional non-US registries.* | Web search, bulk download, or REST API: GET https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies with query parameters — no API key required; JSON responses. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**DailyMed**](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov) | NLM's authoritative, continuously updated collection of FDA drug product labels (package inserts) in structured SPL XML format, covering prescription and OTC products. *US labels only. SPL XML takes some parsing effort; pair with RxNorm identifiers for cross-referencing.* | Web search; REST API at https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/services/ (JSON/XML); full-corpus bulk downloads of SPL archives. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**DrugBank**](https://go.drugbank.com) | Curated database of drugs, targets, mechanisms, interactions and chemical properties, widely used in pharmacology and drug-repurposing research; web interface is freely browsable. *The full academic download is CC BY-NC — no commercial use — and approval is human-mediated with variable turnaround. Commercial use and the clinical API are paid products.* | Browse free on the web. Full dataset download: apply for the free non-commercial academic license (account creation plus a human-reviewed application). A smaller 'DrugBank Open Data' subset (drug names and identifiers) is CC0 and downloads without approval. | Free tier, application | 3/5 |
| [**Grand Challenge**](https://grand-challenge.org) | Platform for biomedical-imaging machine learning hosting 420 challenges and 7,700+ deployed algorithms, with 135,000+ registered users; run by the Diagnostic Image Analysis Group (Radboud UMC, Nijmegen) since 2012. *Participation is free; organizing a challenge has a pricing policy. Each challenge sets its own data license and rules — some require signed agreements. Online algorithm runs are limited by credits.* | Free account (web); join a challenge to download its training data, submit algorithms as Docker containers, or try published algorithms on your own images in the browser. | Free, email | 3/5 |
| [**Human Mortality Database**](https://www.mortality.org) | Detailed, methodologically uniform death counts, death rates and complete life tables for 40+ countries and regions with reliable vital registration, some series reaching back to the 19th century. Run by UC Berkeley and the Max Planck Institute for Demographic Research. *High-registration-quality countries only — mostly Europe, North America, East Asia; no African coverage. Cite the database and extraction date.* | Free registration (email), then direct download of text tables or bulk zip; R users: install.packages("HMDHFDplus") to pull series by country code. | Free (registration), email | 4/5 |
| [**IHME GBD Results Tool**](https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results/) | Query and download estimates from the Global Burden of Disease study (GBD 2023): 375 diseases and injuries, 88 risk factors, 204 countries and territories, annual estimates 1990-2023 (deaths, DALYs, YLDs, prevalence, incidence). *Free for non-commercial use only (IHME's license). These are model-based estimates, not raw surveillance data — cite the GBD round and uncertainty intervals. Full-precision results only via download, not the on-screen table.* | Web interface at vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results — build a query, download CSV; large extractions are emailed as a download link. Free IHME account required to download. | Free (registration), email | 4/5 |
| [**MedMNIST**](https://medmnist.com) | 18 standardized, lightweight biomedical image classification datasets: 12 2D sets totaling 708,069 images and 6 3D sets totaling 9,998 volumes (28x28 or 28x28x28; MedMNIST+ adds sizes up to 224x224), spanning X-ray, OCT, ultrasound, pathology, CT and more. *Deliberately small images — ideal for prototyping and teaching on a laptop, not for state-of-the-art clinical claims. Individual datasets carry their own CC licenses (a few are non-commercial).* | pip install medmnist, then e.g. from medmnist import PathMNIST; data auto-download with train/val/test splits and baseline code on GitHub (MedMNIST/MedMNIST). | Free | 5/5 |
| [**MIMIC-IV**](https://physionet.org/content/mimiciv/) | De-identified electronic health records from Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center (Boston): version 3.1 covers 364,627 patients, 546,028 hospitalizations and 94,458 ICU stays, with labs, medications, notes (MIMIC-IV-Note) and linked chest X-rays (MIMIC-CXR, ~377,000 images). *Credentialed access only (free CITI course via the MIT-affiliates route, ID details, DUA). Data may not be shared onward or used to attempt re-identification; LLM-API use of the text notes is restricted by the DUA. Full local copy is large — BigQuery avoids the download.* | Complete PhysioNet credentialing plus the MIMIC data use agreement, then download CSVs or query the hosted copy on Google BigQuery (physionet-data project); community code at github.com/MIT-LCP/mimic-code. | Free, credentialing | 2/5 |
| [**NCI Genomic Data Commons (GDC)**](https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov) | NCI's unified repository for cancer genomics, hosting TCGA (~11,000 cases across 33 cancer types), TARGET, CPTAC and other programs: mutations, expression, methylation, clinical data. *The open tier alone supports substantial research. Controlled access requires a dbGaP application through an eRA Commons account with an institutional signing official — effectively out of reach for unaffiliated researchers. NIH restricts controlled-data access from designated 'countries of concern' (policy since April 2025).* | Open-access tier (masked somatic mutations, gene expression, clinical data) downloads directly from the portal, the gdc-client transfer tool, or the REST API at api.gdc.cancer.gov — no account needed. Controlled-access tier (raw sequence, germline) requires dbGaP authorization. | Free | 3/5 |
| [**NHANES**](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/) | US National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey: interview, physical examination and laboratory microdata in continuous 2-year cycles since 1999 (plus historical NHANES I-III), ~10,000 participants per cycle. *Analyses must use the complex survey weights (svy commands / R survey package) or results will be wrong. A small set of restricted variables (e.g. fine geography) requires an NCHS Research Data Center application.* | Direct download of SAS transport (XPT) files per cycle and component — no registration; in R, the nhanesA package (install.packages("nhanesA")) pulls tables and documentation directly. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**openFDA**](https://open.fda.gov) | FDA's public API over adverse event reports (FAERS drug events, MAUDE device events), drug product labels, recalls and enforcement actions, NDC directory, and more, with harmonized JSON records. *Without a key: 240 requests/min and 1,000/day per IP; with a free key: 240/min and 120,000/day. Adverse-event data are spontaneous reports — no denominators, well-known reporting biases; openFDA itself warns against causal inference.* | REST API at https://api.fda.gov (e.g. /drug/event.json?search=...); no key needed for light use; free API key raises the daily quota. Bulk downloads of each endpoint also available. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**OpenPrescribing**](https://openprescribing.net) | Monthly primary-care prescribing data for every NHS general practice in England, searchable by drug, practice and region, with dashboards flagging cost-saving and safety measures. Built by the Bennett Institute for Applied Data Science, University of Oxford. *England only; prescribing (dispensed items and cost), not diagnoses or outcomes. Practice-level, already aggregated — no patient microdata.* | Web interface with CSV export; REST API at https://openprescribing.net/api/1.0/ (no key); underlying raw data downloadable in full. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**PhysioNet**](https://physionet.org) | Repository of hundreds of clinical and physiological datasets (EHR, ICU waveforms, ECG, chest X-rays) run by the MIT Laboratory for Computational Physiology, including the MIMIC family and the eICU Collaborative Research Database. Datasets are tiered as open, restricted, or credentialed. *Open-tier datasets (e.g. many waveform databases) need no credentialing at all. Credentialing is free but takes days to process; each protected dataset additionally requires signing its own DUA. No institutional affiliation is required — independent researchers can complete the whole route.* | Web download or Google Cloud/BigQuery. For credentialed datasets: create a PhysioNet account, complete the free CITI 'Data or Specimens Only Research' course (register under the 'Massachusetts Institute of Technology Affiliates' group to avoid CITI fees), upload the training report, apply for credentialing (students name a supervisor as reference), then sign the per-dataset data use agreement. | Free, credentialing | 3/5 |
| [**RxNorm / UMLS**](https://uts.nlm.nih.gov) | NLM's Unified Medical Language System links major biomedical vocabularies (SNOMED CT, ICD, LOINC, MeSH); RxNorm is the standard US drug-name vocabulary. Both are free of charge for research. *The UMLS license is free but individual and non-transferable, with an annual usage report; some bundled source vocabularies (e.g. CPT) carry extra restrictions from their owners. SNOMED CT full use is limited to member-country affiliates.* | The RxNav/RxNorm REST API (https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov) is public, no license or key needed, for RxNorm-proper content. Full UMLS and RxNorm file releases require a free UMLS license: apply via a UMLS Terminology Services account (approval typically within ~5 business days). | Free (registration), application | 3/5 |
| [**SEER**](https://seer.cancer.gov) | NCI's Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results program: cancer incidence, treatment and survival data from US registries covering roughly 48% of the US population, from 1975 onward. *The microdata route effectively requires an institutional email (.edu/.gov/.org or work address) and, for Research Plus, an institutional signing official — hard for unaffiliated researchers, who still get real value from SEER*Explorer. Since April 2025 NIH blocks access from designated 'countries of concern'.* | SEER*Explorer (web statistics tool) is fully open. Research microdata: request access at seer.cancer.gov/data/, authenticate with an eRA Commons or HHS login tied to an institutional email, sign the data use agreement, then analyze in the free SEER*Stat desktop tool. | Free, credentialing | 2/5 |
| [**The Cancer Imaging Archive (TCIA)**](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net) | NCI-funded archive of hundreds of de-identified cancer imaging collections (CT, MRI, PET, digital pathology), many with linked clinical data, segmentations and genomics (e.g. TCGA imaging counterparts). Operating since 2011. *Most collections are fully public with no account; a minority are 'limited access' and require an account plus per-collection approval. Collections are large (many GB) — download selectively. Licenses vary by collection (most CC BY).* | Browse collections on the web portal; download DICOM via the NBIA Data Retriever desktop tool, or programmatically via the NBIA REST API (Python wrapper: pip install tcia_utils). | Free | 4/5 |
| [**The DHS Program**](https://dhsprogram.com) | Demographic and Health Surveys: nationally representative household survey microdata (fertility, child health, nutrition, HIV, mortality) from 90+ low- and middle-income countries, collected since 1984. *USAID terminated the program during the agency's 2025 shutdown; it was announced as continuing in January 2026 and registrations and downloads have resumed, but long-term funding remains uncertain — download and archive what you need. Access is granted per project; redistribution is not permitted.* | Free registration with a short project description, then download survey datasets (Stata/SPSS/flat formats); API at api.dhsprogram.com for indicator data. IPUMS-DHS offers harmonized extracts under the same approval. | Free (registration), email | 3/5 |
| [**UK Biobank**](https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk) | Prospective cohort of ~500,000 UK participants with genotypes, whole-genome sequencing, imaging subsets, biomarkers and linked health records, accessed via the cloud-based Research Analysis Platform (UKB-RAP on DNAnexus). *Not free for most users: standard institutional access fees are substantially higher than the £500 concession, and RAP compute/storage is pay-as-you-go beyond credits (though ~90% of projects reportedly stay under £1,000). Applicants must qualify as bona fide researchers, which in practice favors institutional affiliation; a fund covers 100% of application costs for researchers at institutes in eligible lower-income countries, with additional AWS credits for early-career and LMIC researchers.* | Register as a researcher via the Access Management System, submit an application, pay the access fee, then analyze in UKB-RAP. Reduced tier for students and researchers in eligible lower-income countries: £500 for 3 years; new projects currently receive £1,000 in platform credits (offer runs until Summer 2026) plus £40 on joining. | Freemium, application | 1/5 |
| [**Vivli**](https://vivli.org) | Nonprofit repository brokering anonymized individual-participant data from thousands of completed clinical trials contributed by pharmaceutical companies, foundations and academic groups, for secondary analysis and meta-analysis. *Requesting data is free, but approval rests with each data contributor and takes weeks to months; the secure research environment is free only for an initial period, after which nominal charges apply. Realistic for a well-defined reanalysis project, not for browsing.* | Search the index freely; submit a research proposal with an analysis plan through the platform; on approval and signing the data use agreement, analyze in Vivli's secure research environment or (for some datasets) download. | Free, application | 1/5 |
| [**WHO Global Health Observatory (GHO)**](https://www.who.int/data/gho) | WHO's repository of 1,000+ health indicators (mortality, disease burden, health systems, risk factors) for its 194 member states, with time series. *Country-level estimates only (no microdata); indicator update cadence varies. Indicator codes are cryptic — start from the /api/Indicator dimension listing.* | OData REST API at https://ghoapi.azureedge.net/api (e.g. /api/Indicator to list, /api/WHOSIS_000001 for life expectancy) — no key; also CSV/Excel downloads from the web portal. | Free | 4/5 |

## Software

| Resource | What it is | Access | Free | Beginner |

| [**3D Slicer**](https://www.slicer.org) | Free, open-source desktop application for visualization, segmentation and analysis of medical images (DICOM-native), developed continuously since 1998 with NIH support; over 150 extensions including AI segmentation (MONAI Label, TotalSegmentator). *Permissive BSD-style license with no usage restrictions, but it is explicitly not FDA-approved for clinical use — research only. Comfortable on an ordinary laptop; deep-learning extensions benefit from a GPU.* | Direct download for Windows/macOS/Linux from download.slicer.org; scriptable in Python from the built-in console; extensions installed from the in-app Extensions Manager. | Free | 4/5 |

Final version 7.2.7 (March 2025). *CDC discontinued product development and technical support after September 1, 2025 — downloads remain available but future Windows/.NET updates may break it. Fine for coursework and small studies today; do not build long-term infrastructure on it.* | Direct download from the CDC site; no registration. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**G*Power**](https://www.psychologie.hhu.de/arbeitsgruppen/allgemeine-psychologie-und-arbeitspsychologie/gpower) | Free desktop program (Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf) for a priori and post hoc power analysis across t-tests, ANOVA, regression, chi-square and more — the tool most cited in clinical-research sample-size sections. *Not open source (free binary only) and updated infrequently; for complex designs (clustered trials, survival) you will need R packages instead.* | Direct download for Windows/macOS from the HHU page; no registration. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**ITK-SNAP**](http://www.itksnap.org) | Free, open-source tool focused on one job: segmenting structures in 3D/4D medical images (MRI, CT), with semi-automatic active-contour segmentation plus manual editing. NIH-funded, maintained by UPenn and collaborators. *Deliberately narrower than 3D Slicer — easier to learn for pure segmentation work, less suited to complex pipelines.* | Direct download for Windows/macOS/Linux from itksnap.org; reads NIfTI and DICOM. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**KoboToolbox**](https://www.kobotoolbox.org) | Open-source survey and field-data-collection platform (offline-capable Android app plus web forms) widely used in humanitarian and global-health research; free Community Plan includes 5,000 submissions/month, 1 GB storage and unlimited projects. *Free plan limits reset monthly; heavier use needs a paid plan or self-hosting the open-source stack. Ethical/IRB responsibilities for human-subjects data remain yours — the tool does not confer approval.* | Sign up at kobotoolbox.org (nonprofit Community Plan); build XLSForm surveys in the browser; collect offline with the KoboCollect app; export CSV/XLSX or pull via the REST API. | Free tier, email | 4/5 |
| [**metafor (R package)**](https://www.metafor-project.org) | The standard open-source R package for meta-analysis: fixed/random-effects and multilevel models, meta-regression, forest, funnel and many diagnostic plots; extensively documented with worked reproductions of textbook examples. *Requires basic R. Together with Rayyan (screening) and PROSPERO (registration) it forms a complete zero-cost systematic-review toolchain.* | install.packages("metafor") in R; rma(yi, vi, data=...) fits a random-effects model; documentation and examples at metafor-project.org. | Free | 3/5 |
| [**MONAI**](https://monai.io) | PyTorch-based open-source framework (Apache 2.0) for deep learning in healthcare imaging: medical-specific transforms, network architectures, a Model Zoo of pretrained bundles, and MONAI Label for AI-assisted annotation inside 3D Slicer. *Training real models needs a GPU — pairs naturally with free tiers like Colab/Kaggle for small experiments. Originated by NVIDIA and King's College London; community support via GitHub Discussions.* | pip install monai; tutorials at github.com/Project-MONAI/tutorials; pretrained models via the MONAI Model Zoo (monai.bundle API). | Free | 3/5 |
| [**OHDSI / OMOP**](https://www.ohdsi.org) | Open community standard (OMOP Common Data Model) plus a fully open-source toolchain — ATLAS for cohort building, the HADES R packages for characterization, population-level estimation and prediction — used by a global network of health databases. *The software is free but you must bring your own patient-level data mapped to OMOP — the tools do not include data. A few Athena vocabularies (e.g. CPT4) require entering UMLS credentials. Steep but well-documented learning curve; 'The Book of OHDSI' is free online.* | Code on github.com/OHDSI (ATLAS, HADES: install via R remotes from GitHub); standardized vocabularies downloaded free from https://athena.ohdsi.org after creating an account. | Free, email | 2/5 |
| [**OpenEpi**](https://www.openepi.com) | Free, open-source web calculators for epidemiology: sample size, power, confidence intervals, 2x2 tables, rates, stratified analysis (Mantel-Haenszel) and more; runs entirely in the browser. *Calculator-level tool, not a data-analysis environment; interface is dated but the statistics are solid and documented.* | Web interface at openepi.com; downloadable for offline use. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**Rayyan**](https://www.rayyan.ai) | Web tool for collaborative title/abstract screening in systematic reviews: blind dual screening, deduplication, keyword highlighting. Free tier includes up to 3 concurrent reviews with collaborators. *Free tier lacks the PRISMA flow diagram, automatic duplicate resolution and the newer AI features; unlimited reviews and AI assistance require paid plans (individual plans from roughly $5-8/month).* | Sign up at rayyan.ai, import RIS/EndNote/CSV search exports, invite co-reviewers, screen in browser or mobile app. | Free tier, email | 5/5 |
| [**REDCap**](https://projectredcap.org) | The de facto standard web application for building clinical study databases and surveys (Vanderbilt-led), used by thousands of consortium partner institutions in over 150 countries. *Honest boundary: individuals cannot license REDCap — no institution, no REDCap (REDCap Cloud is a separate commercial product). If your institution lacks it, a nonprofit institution can join the consortium at no cost; unaffiliated researchers should look at KoboToolbox or LimeSurvey instead.* | License is free but granted only to nonprofit organizations that join the REDCap consortium and self-host; researchers then get accounts through their institution's REDCap instance. | Free, application | 3/5 |

## Literature

| Resource | What it is | Access | Free | Beginner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [**Cochrane Library**](https://www.cochranelibrary.com) | The reference collection of systematic reviews in healthcare (thousands of Cochrane Reviews plus the CENTRAL trials register). Free one-click access in 126 low- and middle-income countries via IP recognition, and via national licenses in many others. *Full review texts remain paywalled for individual users in countries without a national or LMIC arrangement (the plan to flip fully open by 2025 was not achieved); many reviews become free one year after publication. CENTRAL is a strong free trials-search source in eligible countries.* | Web interface; check cochranelibrary.com/help/access for whether your country has free or national access. Abstracts and plain-language summaries are free worldwide; editorials and new review protocols are open access (CC BY) from 2025 onward. | Free tier | 4/5 |
| [**Epistemonikos**](https://www.epistemonikos.org) | The largest free database of health systematic reviews — over 300,000 systematic reviews plus their included primary studies, interlinked so you can pivot from a question to all relevant syntheses. *Best used for scoping what evidence syntheses already exist before starting your own review; links out to full text, which may or may not be free.* | Web search (free account for saving and some features); interface in multiple languages. | Free, email | 4/5 |
| [**Europe PMC**](https://europepmc.org) | European counterpart and superset of PMC: abstracts plus millions of full-text articles, with preprints (including medRxiv/bioRxiv) indexed alongside journal articles, citation links, and text-mined annotations of genes, diseases and chemicals. *The single best free programmatic route into medical full text; ORCID login (optional) enables claiming your own articles.* | Web search; REST API at https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/ (no key); Annotations API for mined entities; full open-access corpus downloadable. | Free | 4/5 |
| [**PubMed**](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) | NLM's free index of over 38 million biomedical citations and abstracts (MEDLINE core), with MeSH-indexed search — the central literature database of medicine. *Citations and abstracts only — full text depends on the journal; follow the LinkOut/PMC links or Europe PMC for free full text. Learn MeSH and field tags early; naive keyword search misses much.* | Web search; programmatic via NCBI E-utilities (eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov — esearch/efetch), Entrez in Biopython, or the annual downloadable MEDLINE/PubMed baseline. Free API key (NCBI account) raises the limit from 3 to 10 requests/second. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**PubMed Central (PMC)**](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) | NLM's free digital archive of over 10 million full-text biomedical articles, including everything published from NIH funding under the public access policy. *All of PMC is free to read, but only the Open Access Subset is licensed for redistribution and bulk text mining — check the license field before mining. Deposit embargoes mean the newest paywalled articles may appear after a delay.* | Read free on the web; bulk text mining via the PMC Open Access Subset (FTP/cloud bulk packages) and the OA Web Service API. | Free | 5/5 |
| [**Research4Life (Hinari)**](https://www.research4life.org) | Publisher partnership giving institutions in ~125 lower-income countries online access to tens of thousands of peer-reviewed journals and e-books; Hinari is the health sciences program (WHO-coordinated). *Institutions only — individuals cannot register. Group A country institutions pay nothing; Group B institutions pay USD 1,500/year. Content access varies by publisher and country group; some publishers exclude their newest issues.* | Your institution (university, teaching hospital, research institute, government office, NGO, national library) registers at research4life.org; all its staff and students then log in through the institutional credentials. | Free, application | 3/5 |
